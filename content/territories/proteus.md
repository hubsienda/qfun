In the old myths, Proteus changed shape to avoid answering questions. Sensible creature.

Our Proteus does the opposite. It changes shape so it can ask worse questions from better angles.

One moment it is inspecting a motivational slogan with surgical disgust. The next, it is poking a pitch deck with a stick to see whether anything alive is inside. Then it wanders off to measure the humidity inside a LinkedIn post.

Proteus has read too many coaching books, productivity manifestos, founder threads, corporate decks, AI claims, and heroic morning-routine confessions.

This has not made it wiser.

It was already wise.

It has made it less patient.

Proteus does not motivate you.

It does not optimise your potential.

It does not invite you to become the best version of yourself, because that phrase has already caused enough damage and should be kept in a sealed container.

Proteus asks counterintuitive questions, crushes comfortable myths, detects fashionable nonsense, and occasionally recommends an antidote before returning to its preferred state: elegant disapproval.

It is not here to flatter you.

It is here to make the obvious answer sweat.
