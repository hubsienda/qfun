# QOOBIX Case Studies

## Purpose of this page

These case studies show how QOOBIX can be used in practical commercial situations.

They are examples only. They are not guarantees, endorsements, or verified market reports. Every generated report must be checked before use.

No real client, supplier, distributor, manufacturer, project, or partner names are used in these examples.

---

# Case Study 1: Building-material manufacturer entering a new market

## Situation

A manufacturer of a technical building product wants to explore opportunities in a target country.

The product is relevant for renovation, energy efficiency, indoor comfort, and specialist installation contexts.

The company already has one existing regional representative in the target country but wants to understand where and how to prioritise commercial action.

## Example request

Market question:

Which regions and commercial channels in the target country should be prioritised for the distribution and specification of this technical building product?

Commercial objective:

Distributor discovery.

## What QOOBIX should help identify

QOOBIX should help identify regions where demand may exist, distributor and installer channels worth validating, customer types that may influence adoption, competitor and substitute categories, risks that should be checked before outreach, and practical next actions.

## Useful outputs

The DOCX report is best for management review. The XLSX workbook is best for organising channels, regions, risks, and verification actions. The RTF report is best for Google Docs. The CSV export is best for Google Sheets.

## How the client should use the report

The client should use the report to create a shortlist of regions, distributors, technical partners, and verification actions.

The report should not be used as a final list of confirmed partners. Every organisation, claim, competitor, and opportunity must be verified.

# Case Study 2: Competitor mapping before entering a target country

## Situation

A technical product company wants to understand the competitor and substitute landscape in a target country before approaching distributors.

The company does not yet know whether it should position the product against premium alternatives, low-cost substitutes, generic solutions, traditional methods, or established incumbent products.

## Example request

Market question:

Which direct competitors, substitute systems, local alternatives, and incumbent solutions should be mapped before positioning this technical product in the target country?

Commercial objective:

Competitor mapping.

## What QOOBIX should help identify

QOOBIX should help identify direct competitor categories, substitute products, local incumbent solutions, buyer workarounds, positioning risks, claims that need technical validation, possible channel conflicts, and verification priorities.

## How the output helps

The report can help the client avoid entering the market with vague or weak positioning.

Instead of saying “we compete with similar products”, the client can separate direct technical competitors, substitute systems, lower-cost alternatives, do-nothing behaviour, and familiar products used because they are already known by the market.

## Recommended follow-up

After reading the report, the client should check competitor websites, review distributor catalogues, compare technical claims, check certificates and regulations, speak with installers or technical specifiers, and refine sales positioning.

# Case Study 3: Distributor discovery for a specialist food product

## Situation

A producer of a specialist food product wants to enter a new country.

The company needs to identify likely routes to market before spending money on trade fairs, agents, or direct outreach.

## Example request

Market question:

Which distributor types, wholesale channels, retail formats, and regional opportunities should be prioritised for launching this specialist food product in the target country?

Commercial objective:

Distributor discovery.

## Business profile fields that matter

The product or service field should describe product format, target buyer, shelf life, packaging, and price positioning.

The target customer types may include importers, wholesalers, speciality retailers, food-service buyers, gourmet shops, regional distributors, hospitality suppliers, and online retailers.

The target channels may include food distributors, importers, trade fairs, speciality retail, restaurant supply, gourmet e-commerce, and regional wholesalers.

Known competitors may include existing brands, substitute products, supermarket alternatives, local producers, and imported products.

## What QOOBIX should help identify

QOOBIX should help identify distribution channels worth validating, buyer types to prioritise, competitive alternatives, regional or segment opportunities, positioning angles, and practical outreach steps.

## How the client should use the report

The client should use the output to prepare a first validation campaign.

The report should not replace direct buyer conversations, price testing, importer checks, logistics review, labelling review, or regulatory validation.

# Case Study 7: Logistics and transport company finding new B2B sectors

## Situation

A logistics and transport company wants to expand beyond its current client base.

The company provides regional and cross-border transport, scheduled distribution, temperature-controlled handling, warehousing support, or last-mile delivery for business clients.

It wants to identify which sectors, regions, and customer types may offer the best commercial opportunities.

## Example request

Market question:

Which B2B sectors, regional corridors, and customer types should be prioritised for expanding a logistics and transport service in the target market?

Commercial objective:

Market-entry analysis.

## Business profile fields that matter

The product or service field should describe transport types, delivery radius, fleet capability, warehousing capacity, temperature requirements, cross-border ability, delivery frequency, and operational constraints.

Target customer types may include food producers, wholesalers, retailers, manufacturers, importers, exporters, e-commerce operators, packaging companies, event suppliers, hospitality suppliers, and industrial distributors.

Target channels may include direct sales, sector associations, logistics tenders, distributor networks, procurement portals, trade fairs, and regional business clusters.

## What QOOBIX should help identify

QOOBIX should help identify sectors with transport demand, underserved routes, buyer types with recurring logistics needs, competitor categories, partner opportunities, tender or procurement routes, and risks such as seasonality, compliance, pricing pressure, and capacity mismatch.

## How the client should use the report

The client should use the report to shortlist target sectors, prepare sector-specific outreach, review route economics, validate demand through direct conversations, and avoid pursuing sectors that look attractive but do not match operational capacity.

# Case Study 9: Fruit and vegetable producer expanding international sales

## Situation

A company produces fruit and vegetables and already sells into several international markets.

It wants to identify additional commercial opportunities in European countries and North America, while considering seasonality, perishability, importer relationships, certifications, logistics, packaging, and buyer requirements.

## Example request

Market question:

Which buyer types, import channels, regional markets, and commercial risks should be prioritised for expanding fruit and vegetable sales across selected European and North American markets?

Commercial objective:

Regional opportunity assessment.

## Business profile fields that matter

The product or service field should describe product categories, seasonality, packaging formats, certifications, production capacity, export experience, cold-chain requirements, and target buyer types.

Target customer types may include importers, wholesale markets, fresh-produce distributors, supermarket buyers, food-service suppliers, processors, hospitality suppliers, and specialist retailers.

Target channels may include importers, produce wholesalers, distributors, supermarket procurement, food-service supply chains, trade fairs, fresh-produce marketplaces, and direct buyer outreach.

Known competitors may include local producers, importers from competing regions, large grower cooperatives, wholesale-market suppliers, and substitute fresh-produce categories.

## What QOOBIX should help identify

QOOBIX should help identify promising market segments, buyer categories, seasonal windows, possible channel routes, competitor pressure, packaging or certification expectations, logistics risks, and verification priorities.

## How the client should use the report

The client should use the report to select a few target markets for deeper validation, not to launch everywhere at once.

The next steps should include checking import rules, certification requirements, cold-chain feasibility, buyer specifications, seasonality overlap, price expectations, and distributor credibility.
