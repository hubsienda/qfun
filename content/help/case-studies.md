# Case Studies

These case studies are fictional examples. They are designed to show how QOOBIX can support different types of commercial decisions.

They do not describe real clients.

## Case Study 1: Market entry for a technical food-safety product

### Situation

A company has developed a compact monitoring device for food storage rooms. It wants to understand whether entering a new European market is commercially sensible.

### QOOBIX mode

Analysis Mode.

### Request type

Market-entry analysis.

### Market question

Which customer segments, commercial channels, and early validation steps should be prioritised for entering the target country with a compact food-storage monitoring device?

### How QOOBIX helps

QOOBIX analyses likely buyer categories, channel routes, purchasing concerns, substitutes, market-entry risks, and first validation steps.

The output helps the company avoid treating the whole country as one market. It separates possible early adopters from weaker segments and identifies what must be checked before investing in outreach.

### Likely outputs

The report may identify priority customer types, channel opportunities, competitor and substitute categories, regional or segment priorities, positioning recommendations, commercial risks, and a practical action matrix.

### Decision supported

Whether the company should proceed with market validation, which channels should be tested first, and what evidence is needed before committing resources.

## Case Study 2: Distributor discovery for an agricultural product

### Situation

A producer of specialist agricultural testing kits wants to find possible routes into a foreign market. It does not yet know which distributors or specialist resellers may be relevant.

### QOOBIX mode

Discovery Mode.

### Request type

Distributor discovery.

### Market question

Which distributors, specialist resellers, agricultural suppliers, or related market actors should be discovered and verified as possible routes to market?

### How QOOBIX helps

QOOBIX uses controlled discovery to identify named candidate organisations. It then organises them as candidates for verification and adds commercial reasoning around why they may be relevant.

The report does not treat the candidates as confirmed distributors. Instead, it gives the client a structured starting list for checking location, service scope, customer fit, product compatibility, and contact route.

### Likely outputs

The report may include a candidate organisation list, verification routes, relevance explanations, suggested actions, channel analysis, risks, and a spreadsheet suitable for filtering and follow-up planning.

### Decision supported

Which candidate distributors or resellers should be checked first, and whether the market has enough plausible channel actors to justify deeper commercial work.

## Case Study 3: Competitor and substitute mapping for a software product

### Situation

A company sells specialist operations software for independent service businesses. It wants to enter a new country but does not know the competitive landscape.

### QOOBIX mode

Discovery Mode.

### Request type

Competitor mapping.

### Market question

Which direct competitors, substitutes, local alternatives, and status-quo solutions should be mapped before positioning the product in the target country?

### How QOOBIX helps

QOOBIX does not look only for obvious direct competitors. It also considers substitutes such as generic software, internal manual processes, spreadsheets, local consultants, or embedded tools already used by buyers.

Discovery Mode can provide named candidate organisations where relevant. Analysis then explains how those candidates fit into the wider competitive environment.

### Likely outputs

The report may include competitor rows, substitute categories, positioning risks, buyer objections, channel implications, verification routes, and action priorities.

### Decision supported

How to position the product, which competitors or alternatives must be checked, and what claims should be avoided until more evidence is collected.

## Case Study 8: Fresh produce export market assessment

### Situation

A company produces fruit and vegetables and sells to several international markets. It wants to assess a new country or channel without relying only on existing contacts.

### QOOBIX mode

Analysis Mode or Discovery Mode, depending on the need.

### Request type

Market-entry analysis, channel analysis, or prospect discovery.

### Market question

Which buyer types, commercial channels, and verification steps should be prioritised for expanding fresh produce sales into the target market?

### How QOOBIX helps

In Analysis Mode, QOOBIX can structure the market by buyer type, import route, wholesale channel, retail route, foodservice opportunity, logistics constraint, and commercial risk.

In Discovery Mode, QOOBIX can identify candidate wholesalers, distributors, importers, market operators, or relevant service providers for verification.

### Likely outputs

The report may include channel opportunities, buyer categories, regional or port-related priorities, competitor and substitute considerations, logistics constraints, verification routes, and an action matrix.

### Decision supported

Which market route should be tested first, whether named candidate organisations should be verified, and what operational constraints must be checked before outreach.

## Case Study 9: Commercial channel selection for a technical product

## Case Study 10: Prospect discovery for a niche digital service

### Situation

A business sells a niche digital service to small and medium organisations. It wants to find early candidate prospects in a target country, but the market is fragmented.

### QOOBIX mode

Discovery Mode.

### Request type

Lead/prospect discovery.

### Market question

Which categories of organisations in the target country should be discovered and verified as possible early prospects for this niche digital service?

### How QOOBIX helps

QOOBIX identifies named candidate organisations where possible and groups them by relevance. It also explains why each type of candidate may or may not fit the commercial objective.

The output is not a finished sales list. It is a structured verification list that helps the client decide where outreach might begin.

### Likely outputs

The report may include candidate organisations, verification routes, candidate status, suggested actions, prospect categories, risks, and spreadsheet outputs for filtering.

### Decision supported

Which candidate prospects should be checked first, what qualification questions should be asked, and whether the target segment is worth pursuing.

## Final note

QOOBIX is most useful when it is used as a commercial thinking system, not as a magic list machine.

Analysis Mode helps clarify the decision.

Discovery Mode helps create candidate material for verification.

The value comes from combining both: structured reasoning, named candidates where appropriate, editable outputs, and disciplined follow-up.

The glamorous fantasy is that software knows the market.

The practical truth is better: QOOBIX helps you ask sharper questions, find better starting points, and avoid wandering into the commercial fog with a confident expression and no map.
